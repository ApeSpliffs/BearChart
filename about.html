How BearChart prices things

BearChart is a research tool for collectors and resellers. It answers what did this actually sell for, not what is someone asking. Those are different questions, and conflating them is how people end up holding a bear they paid $200 for because one listing said $200.

The one rule

Completed sold transactions outrank active asking prices — always. An asking price is a hope. A sold price is evidence. BearChart's engine refuses to treat active_listing or retail_reference rows as market evidence, and there is a test that fails the build if that ever changes.

Where a number comes from

  1. Collect every sold observation for the exact item — versions and variants are kept apart, never averaged together.
  2. Normalise each sale to a common baseline (Good condition, no extras) using the configured condition and completeness multipliers.
  3. Drop outliers beyond 1.5× the interquartile range — but only once there are at least five sales. With three, the odd one out is often the real market.
  4. Take the median, never the mean and never a single sale.
  5. Publish the 25th–75th percentile as the working range; below four comps, show the full observed spread instead, because quartiles of three points are noise.
  6. Derive buy, trade and high-end ask as configured percentages of that value.
  7. Assign confidence from the number of comparable sales.

Confidence

Comparable salesConfidenceWhat it means
10 or moreHIGH A real market exists. Price with confidence.
4–9MEDIUM Directionally sound. Check the condition split.
1–3LOW Anecdote, not a market. Treat as a starting point.
NoneVERY LOW No value is published at all.

What BearChart will not do

  • Invent a price for an item with no sold data. Those items say "no sold data yet" and stay that way until a sourced comp arrives.
  • Turn a current retail price into a resale value. Retail is shown separately, labelled as a baseline only.
  • Average an original release together with a Vault reissue, or one version with another.
  • Present a collector report as a verified completed sale. A sale only earns completed_sale when it carries a date, a marketplace and a listing id.
  • Blindly apply a rarity multiplier. A $250 vintage employee exclusive and a $12 Mini Bean do not obey the same formula.
